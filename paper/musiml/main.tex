\begin{abstract}
We propose two tasks for checking Quran recitation from ASR: detecting and splitting ayahs, and labeling localized transcript/reference events. Task A has a public 258-recording benchmark. For Task B, we completed a private annotation set of 100 human-approved recording cases comprising 314 ayah chunks. Algorithms receive reviewed splits and references. An agent track will annotate a separate unlabelled development pool and build an algorithm for private evaluation. We report annotation findings, distinguish the legacy machine-labeled pilot from the completed gold review, and document input exposure that prevents claiming an unseen-input test.
\end{abstract}

\section{Task and motivation}
Quran memorization involves a listener checking recitation. An ASR-based application must handle opening formulas, repeats, restarts, repairs, and missing or substituted words. Edit distance alone does not distinguish these cases. Our labels assess transcript/reference differences, not whether the speaker or ASR caused them; unwritten vowel and tajweed errors are outside scope.

In \textbf{\taskA\ (detect and split)}, a full transcript maps to ayah IDs and word assignments, or abstention on non-Quran input. The public v1.1 benchmark exists. In \textbf{\taskB\ (event annotation)}, outputs have combined labels such as \texttt{substitution\_mistake} and \texttt{repetition\_benign}, with hypothesis/reference spans. Review covers whole recordings and opening formulas. Algorithms receive reviewed chunks, ayah IDs and exact references; scoring excludes detection errors.

The agent track supplies unlabelled development transcripts, the Quran reference, definitions and disjoint examples. Entrants create annotations and an algorithm; final code is scored. Agreement with self-generated labels is not independent annotation accuracy. Gold performance assesses the combined process, not every development annotation or annotation's causal contribution.

\section{Data and findings from human review}
The public \taskA\ release has 258 transcripts: 254 Quranic recordings, four non-Quran negatives and 1{,}267 reviewed chunks covering 604 ayahs from 45 surahs. Its train/test split has 151/107 recordings and 711/556 chunks. This is a public benchmark; duplicates and related inputs must stay in one partition.

\textbf{Completed \taskB\ review.} As of 8 September 2026, all \textbf{100 selected recording cases} are human-approved: 314 chunks, 233 no-event chunks, 115 within-ayah events, and 47 opening-formula annotations. Three approved reserves are outside the scored set. The selection covers 274 ayahs in 38 surahs and ten learner IDs; one learner contributes 46 recordings. These purposive counts do not estimate prevalence or imply unseen-reciter generalization. The rubric is v0.19; the evaluator and metric are not frozen.

We verified recording IDs and transcripts against both source exports. Punctuation-normalized duplicates and redundant clean subpassages are excluded. Shared passages remain only for documented differences in event pattern or clean/error contrasts. Distinct IDs and paths do not establish different audio takes.

The review settled four rules. \textbf{Omissions} include missing beginnings and endings, with no benign exemption for a final ayah. Whole-ayah omissions require a reviewed continuous-passage interpretation; a completed final ayah does not imply future omissions. \textbf{Repeats} span the original phrase and its extra copy against one reference copy. Wrong-then-correct attempts remain corrected events; a correct-then-incorrect restatement is \texttt{substitution\_mistake} spanning both attempts. \textbf{Spelling} differences handled by agreed normalization receive no event; contextual residual forms use \texttt{spelling\_benign}, with wasl explained in the note. This permits no blanket final-letter deletion. \textbf{Localization} uses verbatim words and half-open spans in original token arrays, including empty hypothesis spans for omissions. Merge continuous replacements, but preserve separate events across matching text or attempt boundaries.

Opening formulas and complete letter-name representations receive benign labels. Possible ASR origin does not create an uncertain verdict.

\section{Evaluation and feasibility evidence}
The existing \taskA\ scalar sums detection, split, and abstention errors. Earlier work reported held-out scores of 0.760 for the incumbent and 0.079 for the best of six coding-agent runs.\footnote{\emph{Autoresearch with coding agents: generalizers and metric-maximizers on Quran recitation data}, AIST 2026. These are earlier Task A results, not Task B experiments.}

The legacy \taskB\ pilot has 99 machine-labeled chunks from 20 recordings, pending human review under its old schema. Its public-test scores were 2.000 for an empty baseline and 1.111 for naive diff and the incumbent. Its separate type/verdict fields, uncertainty category and superseded rules do not validate the new gold. The documented export mismatch---1{,}268 Task B records versus 1{,}267 Task A chunks---is preserved in the historical release.

For the new protocol, freeze matching, scalar weights, the supplied-split interface, budgets, and hypotheses before runs. Report label and localization quality, missed mistakes, false flags on clean/benign/corrected cases, and per-label results. Matching must handle omission anchors and both-attempt spans, and prevent one broad prediction from earning credit for several distinct gold events. Re-run baselines under the frozen contract; no new Task B algorithm results are claimed.

\section{Competition plan and exposure controls}
Protect the completed gold snapshot and prepare a separate unlabelled development bundle, excluding selected inputs and duplicate-equivalent material. Supply disjoint or synthetic examples and a fixed clarification exercise before measured runs. Agents receive no gold answers or gold-score feedback; final code is evaluated privately. Exclude reviewer files, source repositories, answer-bearing history, and annotation-session memory from agent runtimes.

\textbf{Exposure limitation.} All 100 selected transcripts match public Task A inputs; twelve also match transcripts represented in the public legacy Task B labelled pilot. New adjudications and selected membership remain private, but the inputs are not unpublished. Runtime isolation must exclude public-release retrieval and cannot itself establish absence of prior exposure. An unseen-input final competition ranking requires a separate collection of previously unreleased recordings, with reuse permission and de-identification verified before launch. That collection is planned, not already available.

Shared docs contain aggregate findings and synthetic examples; gold answers stay in a separate private reviewer bundle. Verify privacy and reuse scope before new publication.
